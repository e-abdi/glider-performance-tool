Seaglider Endurance Calculation

Predicts how long a Seaglider will last on a given mission. Kongsberg publishes no endurance tool for firmware 66.12, so this one is fitted to our own gliders' logs: every dive's per-device on-times and currents from SG644 (2018, 2019, 2022) and SG150 (2021), 1,500+ dives in all. Run against those dives, it reproduces each mission's logged energy to within a few percent (see How well it fits).

Where the energy goes

How well it fits

Each logged dive run through the model using only what a pilot plans with: the dive's depth and duration, surface time, $MAX_BUOY, $SM_CC, the data it sent and its sensor settings. The model's total against what the glider logged, mission by mission.

What the logs showed

The glider's battery counter leaves the PAM out

SG644's $24V_AH counter books every device except the PAM recorder. The CF card's BATTERY tally for mission 5 (to 3 July 2018) lists 45 Ah drawn by the PAM, and the counter's 247.6 Ah equals the tally without it. The recorder's PAM.CNF puts it on the 15 V main line, so the pack gave ~293 Ah of 310 while the glider reported 248.

This calculator counts the PAM. When a PAM is fitted, add its time to the glider's counter before trusting the remaining capacity.

SciCon or TT8: which is cheaper

With the CT, optode and SeaOWL on the SciCon sampling every ~10 s, the SciCon averaged 25 mA for all of it. Polled by the TT8 instead, the glider charges each sensor its .cnf current for every second it is powered, and keeps the TT8 awake ~3 s per sensor-second. The Contros optode's .cnf says 300 mA for 2.8 s a sample: 845 mAs, ~3.6× what the SciCon drew for all its sensors over a 10 s cycle. On mission 6's dives, without the PAM, that is ~88 days on the SciCon against ~44 on the TT8.

The SciCon reports its own consumption; the TT8 figure is the glider's bookkeeping. Part of the gap may be a conservative .cnf rating, so a bench measurement of the optode would settle it. Until then, edit a sensor's mA to try a measured value.

What moves endurance most

The echosounder. In 2022 it logged 400 mA for the whole dive: 0.77 Ah per dive, more than everything else together, which is why that mission ran at 16.5 Ah/day against 2.6–4.5 in the SciCon years.

The pump, then depth. A cc pumped at apogee costs 13 J plus 0.021 J per metre of depth, so ~15 J at 100 m and ~34 J at 1000 m. Volume is (MAX_BUOY + SM_CC) per dive. SG644 pumped 1.22× that in 2022, at a lower pack voltage (12.4 V against 13.4 V in 2018).

The earlier spreadsheet

endurance calc.xlsx fitted per-device factors to 92 dives of one deployment, and its pump term already scaled with MAX_BUOY + SM_CC. For its own settings (990 m, T_DIVE 420, CT and optode on the SciCon) it and this model agree on the energy of a dive: 0.51 Ah there, 0.50 Ah here.

The endurance differs because it fixed every dive at 2 h. At T_DIVE 420 a 990 m dive takes about 7 h, so 303 Ah lasts ~180 days, not 49. It also scaled Iridium and the motors by 0.5 and the pump by 0.3 by hand, and added the CTD and O2 on top of the SciCon, which already includes them. Those roughly cancelled out.

Missions in the archive

How it is built. code/extract_seaglider_dives.py reduces the deployment archive (the basestation's per-dive netCDF files, 1.6 GB, kept outside the repository) to one row per dive in content/data/seaglider-dives.csv. code/fit_seaglider_model.py fits each group of loads against what drives it: the pump against volume and depth, the motors against dive time, the processor against cycle time, Iridium against data sent, and the SciCon against its sample rate. The fit is written to content/data/seaglider-model.json. node code/build_seaglider_page.js runs code/seaglider_model.js on every dive, fails if any mission misses by more than 25%, and only then updates this page. Fitted on 2018 alone, the model predicted the 2022 mission, with different sensors and no SciCon, to within 7%.

Limits. Everything is calibrated on two gliders on firmware 66.12. Depths beyond 1000 m, and anything past 300 m for SG150, are extrapolation. The SeaOWL's cost on the TT8 is estimated, since it only ever flew on the SciCon. The Kongsberg (iRobot) Seaglider Mission Endurance Estimator user guide (doc. 4366008) was used for parameter names and the rule that a dual-pack glider lasts as long as its weaker pack.
